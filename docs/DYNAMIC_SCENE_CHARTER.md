# Dynamic Scene Charter — AI Simplified Lab

**Status:** Stage 1C production acceptance standard  
**Applies to:** Every future Short and horizontal YouTube video  
**Non-negotiable:** This is a video-production system, not a PowerPoint/slideshow generator.

## Creative principle

Every scene must be conceived from the exact spoken idea it communicates. There is no default card, slide, scene template, static wallpaper, or repeated composition. The topic and narration determine the visual language. The visual language does not dictate the story.

A scene is valid only when it contains a purposeful visual event: an object, process, system, environment, chart, metaphor, or real product interaction that changes meaningfully over time.

## Absolute prohibitions

- No sequence of static slides with text as the primary visual.
- No repeated rounded-card layouts, title-card stacks, generic UI panels or stock backgrounds used as a substitute for visual explanation.
- No scene created merely to fill duration.
- No decorative stock image, icon, sound, music sting, caption or transition without a named role in the scene.
- No generic fade-in-only animation as the sole motion treatment.
- No repeated composition across scenes unless the story deliberately returns to the same object/state and the continuity reason is recorded.
- No made-up UI, fabricated product footage, synthetic metrics presented as real, or screen-demo claims without genuine capture.

## Scene direction requirements

Every scene's direction must define:

1. **Narrative purpose:** the exact viewer question answered by this scene.
2. **Visual intent:** the single idea the viewer should understand even with audio muted.
3. **Focal subject:** the object/system/process that carries the meaning.
4. **Spatial composition:** camera framing, foreground/midground/background, focal hierarchy, negative space and aspect-safe placement. These are designed per scene, not selected from a fixed layout preset.
5. **Visual event:** a beginning, action/change and payoff/resolution. The main visual must evolve, transform, travel, assemble, react, reveal, compare, or otherwise change for a reason.
6. **Motion choreography:** action triggers aligned to voice or sound, target elements, easing/path, duration and cause-and-effect reactions. Camera movement must support the idea rather than act as decoration.
7. **Asset purpose:** every image, clip, diagram, object, texture, chart, logo, icon or footage item must state what it explains or what action it participates in.
8. **Sound design:** narration is the lead. Add scene-specific ambience, impact, transition sounds and music only when they reinforce timing, scale, material, emotion or understanding. Silence may be used deliberately with a recorded reason.
9. **Captions:** voice-aligned word/phrase captions with deliberate emphasis and safe placement. Captions must not hide the focal action.
10. **Transition:** a motivated cut, match-cut, morph, camera continuation, sound bridge or purposeful hard cut. Do not use the same transition mechanically.

## Topic-to-visual modes (not templates)

The director may choose or invent a visual approach per scene, including but not limited to:

- cinematic 3D environment or object choreography;
- physical metaphor with moving parts and real cause/effect;
- data visualization with animated values and spatial relationships;
- process simulation or system-flow animation;
- kinetic typography integrated into the moving scene;
- bespoke SVG/canvas/HTML motion graphics;
- real interface capture for product demonstrations;
- properly licensed footage integrated into a designed composition;
- code or terminal visualization when the code itself is the point;
- hybrid compositions combining multiple techniques.

These are visual techniques, not reusable slide layouts. Two scenes in the same video may use entirely different techniques.

## Sound and voice rules

- One voiceover line must map to each scene or explicitly defined narration segment.
- Voice timing is the timeline's source of truth for visual triggers and captions.
- Do not generate multiple voices or sound layers by default. Pick the voice and sonic identity for the story, then keep them consistent.
- SFX must be synchronized to a visual event and have a purpose such as weight, contact, movement, confirmation, danger, reveal or scale.
- Music must support the emotional arc and be ducked under narration. Do not use one loud loop that competes with speech.
- Store source, terms, license and attribution for all third-party audio and media.

## Acceptance gate

A video is **not accepted** until:

- every scene has a unique, intentional composition (or an explicit continuity reason for a return);
- no scene is a static slide or passive wallpaper;
- every scene has a clear visual event and purposeful motion;
- every asset has an explicit role;
- voice, captions and action cues are synchronized;
- sound design is purposeful and mixed under the voice;
- transitions are motivated;
- actual rendered frames are inspected, not just source code;
- no clipping, unreadable text, broken assets, black gaps, missing audio, subtitle drift or watermark remain;
- output passes the selected format's technical QA.

A technically valid MP4 with repetitive or slide-like scenes fails the creative gate.

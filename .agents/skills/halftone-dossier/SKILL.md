# Halftone Dossier — Evidence as an Editorial Sequence

> Source inspiration: Lemo-Opuscar halftone-dossier — https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/blob/main/styles/halftone-dossier/STYLE.md

## Use this skill when
- A news story has a small number of meaningful documents, figures, claims or evidence items.
- The video needs an editorial collage or printed-case-file visual treatment.
- The story benefits from revealing one exhibit at a time.

## Directing and implementation rules
- Treat each exhibit as evidence supporting a specific claim. Record the source, date, publisher and what the exhibit actually proves.
- Use halftone density, overprint, paper texture, stamps and file numbering as a visual system, not as decoration.
- Reveal exhibits in argumentative order: claim, evidence, context, counterpoint or caveat, implication.
- Distinguish original source material from recreations and illustrative graphics. Never fake an authentic document, screenshot, stamp or quote.
- Use large headlines and a limited number of exhibit labels. All on-screen text must be readable at phone size.

## Workflow
1. Build an evidence ledger before storyboarding; connect each claim to at least one supporting source.
2. Select only exhibits that materially change the viewer's understanding.
3. Design the paper, halftone and type hierarchy once, then create varied exhibit compositions rather than repeated cards.
4. Animate the exhibit itself—unfold, overprint, pin, compare, redact only when genuinely redacted—not arbitrary transitions.
5. Run a factual review of every quote, number, date and source label after rendering.

## Do not
- Fake leaked documents, fabricated screenshots, false confidential markings or misleading archival treatment.
- A repeated sequence of identical evidence cards with only the text changed.
- Using halftone texture to imply documentary truth where the source is an illustration.
- Invented numbers or unsourced claims presented as official exhibits.

## Factory integration
- Keep the editorial palette compatible with AI Simplified Lab's purple collage identity; the technique does not require cream paper.
- Use verified asset IDs and the repository's rights ledger for downloaded source material.
- Keep editorial review and publish approval separate from technical rendering status.

## Acceptance check
- Every exhibit has a traceable source and a clearly stated evidentiary role.
- The sequence is varied and readable, not a static slideshow.
- Recreated or illustrative materials are clearly distinguished from authentic source assets.
